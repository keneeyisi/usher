#pet-lifetime | Lifetime pet cover | levels | Our highest level of cover, renewing every year with a vet fee limit that resets annually. | lifetime pet insurance; cover for life | Insurance that keeps covering my dog's ongoing illness year after year ;; The best policy for a pet that may develop long-term conditions
- What's covered :: Vet fees for illness and injury up to the annual limit, with ongoing conditions covered for as long as you renew.
- Annual limit :: The vet fee limit resets at each renewal, so a long-term condition can continue to be claimed on.
- What's not covered :: Routine care such as vaccinations and neutering, and conditions that existed before cover began.
- Premiums as pets age :: Premiums normally increase as your pet gets older, and a co-payment may apply from age eight.
#pet-time-limited | Time-limited and maximum benefit cover | levels | Lower-cost cover that pays up to a set amount per condition or for a limited period. | maximum benefit; per condition limit; annual vet cover | A cheaper policy that stops paying once the vet bills for one illness reach a limit ;; Cover that only helps for twelve months after a problem starts
- Time-limited cover :: Pays vet fees for a condition for up to 12 months from the date treatment starts, after which it is excluded.
- Maximum benefit cover :: Pays up to a fixed amount per condition; once the limit is reached, no further claims for that condition.
- Which suits your pet :: Younger pets with a short lifespan can suit lower-cost cover; breeds prone to chronic problems may need lifetime.
- Upgrading :: You can usually upgrade at renewal, but conditions that began under the old policy may then be excluded.
#pet-accident-only | Accident-only cover | levels | The most basic level, covering injuries from accidents but not illness. | accident cover; injury only pet insurance | Cheap cover just in case my cat is hit by a car ;; Insurance that pays for injuries but not for illness
- What's covered :: Vet fees for accidental injury such as fractures and wounds, up to the annual limit.
- What's not covered :: Illness, dental disease, and anything not caused by an accident.
- Who it suits :: Owners who want protection from major unexpected costs but accept illness costs themselves.
- Switching to fuller cover :: Moving to lifetime or time-limited cover usually means new waiting periods and exclusions for existing conditions.
#pet-pre-existing | Pre-existing conditions | existing | What counts as a condition your pet already has and how it affects cover. | pet already ill; existing condition; excluded conditions | My dog had a limp before I took out the policy and I want to know if it will be covered ;; Whether illnesses before the policy started can still be claimed
- What counts :: Any illness, injury or symptom your pet has had before the start date, whether or not it was diagnosed.
- How we decide :: We review the vet history for the last 24 months to identify existing conditions.
- Bilateral conditions :: If one side was affected, the other side is excluded too, for example a cruciate ligament in either knee.
- Can an exclusion be removed :: Some conditions can be reviewed after 24 months with no treatment or signs, with a vet's letter.
#pet-waiting-periods | Waiting periods | existing | The time between starting a policy and being able to claim for illness. | cooling off; claim period; when can I claim | I just bought a policy and my puppy is already unwell ;; How many days before I can claim for an illness
- Illness waiting period :: You cannot claim for illness in the first 14 days of the policy; accidents are covered from day one.
- Specific waiting periods :: A cruciate ligament injury may have a 30-day wait, and behavioural therapy a longer wait.
- Continuing from another insurer :: Ask us to accept transfer of your cover with no new waiting periods where eligible.
- Cooling-off :: You can cancel within 14 days of receiving your documents for a full refund if no claim has been made.
#pet-vet-fees | Vet fee limits and excess | vets | The maximum we pay per year or per condition and the excess you share on each claim. | vet bill limit; pet excess; co-payment | The vet says it will cost four thousand pounds and I need to know how much I can claim ;; What I pay myself on each vet bill
- Annual or per-condition limit :: Your schedule shows the maximum payable in a policy year or for each separate condition.
- Excess :: A compulsory excess per claim applies; a voluntary excess lowers your premium but increases what you pay.
- Co-payment for older pets :: From age eight you pay a percentage, usually 10 to 20%, of each claim in addition to the excess.
- Paying the vet directly :: With a direct-payment vet we pay them, leaving you to pay only your share.
#pet-claiming | Claiming for vet fees | vets | How to claim for treatment and what documents are needed. | submit a pet claim; vet invoice; pet claim form | I paid the vet and want my money back ;; Sending in the invoice so I can be refunded for my pet's treatment
- How to claim :: Fill in the online form in My Insura, attach the itemised invoice, and ask your vet to complete their section.
- Direct claims :: Some vets can claim directly on your behalf; check with the practice before treatment.
- How long it takes :: Most straightforward claims are paid within five working days of receiving all documents.
- Claims for ongoing treatment :: Send a new invoice for each visit; we track the total against your annual limit.
#pet-dental-routine | Dental, routine care and breed-specific cover | vets | What is and is not included for teeth, vaccination, neutering and breed-related conditions. | dental cover; vaccinations; neutering; breed conditions | Whether my dog's tooth extraction is covered ;; If routine jabs and flea treatment can be claimed
- Dental treatment :: Dental treatment is covered when it follows an accident or illness; routine scale and polish is not.
- Routine care :: Vaccinations, flea and worming treatments, neutering and microchipping are excluded unless a wellness add-on is chosen.
- Breed-specific conditions :: Some breeds have restrictions on conditions known to be hereditary; see the policy schedule.
- Wellness add-on :: Pays a fixed amount each year towards routine treatments, whether or not your pet is ill.
